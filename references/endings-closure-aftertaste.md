# Endings, Closure, and Aftertaste

Use this reference when designing chapter, arc, volume, or novel endings; when a finale resolves plot but feels emotionally dead; or when an open ending feels merely unfinished.

## Core principle

Closure is multidimensional. A novel may close plot pressure, relation, inquiry, rhythm, image, temporal pattern, experiential movement, or formal sequence while leaving other dimensions open.

Do not require exhaustive resolution or a single "final answer". Project Poetics decides what kind of completion the work promises.

## 1. Closure axes

For the current scale, decide which axes close, transform, or remain open.

Possible axes:

- **event** - what happened in the immediate conflict;
- **causal** - why it happened;
- **epistemic** - what truth is known and by whom;
- **threat** - whether danger remains active;
- **relationship** - what two people can now do or expect from each other;
- **emotional** - whether feeling has settled, changed form, or remains raw;
- **institutional/social** - what the public, law, organization, or community now does;
- **obligation/ethical** - what debt, guilt, duty, promise, or responsibility remains;
- **identity** - who a character now believes themselves to be;
- **thematic** - which central question has been embodied, complicated, or left unresolved;
- **world** - what normality now looks like.

Do not close every axis simply because the book ends.

For a promised fair-clue case, core solution and retrospective solvability must close; interpretive aftertaste may remain. A confession can complete a life trajectory while self-understanding remains incomplete. A memory-led ending can transform the present/past relation. An atmospheric or metaphysical project can complete experience or relationship while ontology remains boundedly open. Compile these independent promises from `project-poetics.md`; openness is not itself an organizing family.

## 2. Open endings need a legible mode of completion, not necessarily resolved plot pressure

An open ending should feel intentional rather than accidentally truncated, but its completion can be:

- experiential;
- relational;
- formal/rhythmic;
- epistemic (including a clarified limit of knowledge);
- thematic without thesis closure;
- situational/local while the wider world continues;
- plot/casual resolution when that is what the project promised.

Do not close a dimension merely because another genre usually would.

## 3. Transformed normality

A powerful ending may return to ordinary life after the story has changed what ordinary life means.

Possible signs:

- the same route is taken differently;
- an old joke no longer lands;
- a room has a new use;
- a routine resumes with one absence;
- a public story differs from private truth;
- two people perform an old habit under a changed relationship;
- the world keeps moving while residue remains.

This often creates stronger aftertaste than a final explanatory speech.

## 4. Retrospective effect

Endings can change how earlier scenes are remembered.

A good final reveal or final image may:

- reclassify an earlier action;
- make a previously ordinary detail emotionally charged;
- reveal the cost of an earlier choice;
- expose the gap between public and private versions;
- complete a pattern without explaining every instance.

Do not use the final paragraph to invalidate objective facts merely for surprise.

## 5. Single landing

Once the ending has landed, stop.

Watch for:

- emotional resolution;
- then thematic explanation;
- then title callback;
- then ominous tag;
- then epilogue sentence explaining the callback.

One strong landing is usually better than several competing landings.

Run the **stop-one-sentence-earlier test**. If the ending gains force, cut.

## 6. Scale-specific endings

### Scene ending
End after the local state change becomes legible.

### Chapter ending
May end on decision, reclassification, procedural result, altered relationship, quiet aftershock, or a new question. Cliffhangers are optional.

### Arc/volume ending
Resolve or transform the governing movement while inheriting meaningful consequences into the next layer.

### Novel ending
The story can leave the world alive. It need not freeze every character into final summary.

Use `long-horizon-architecture.md` when closure at one scale must feed another.

## 7. Epilogues

Use an epilogue only if it adds a distinct temporal or social perspective:

- long-term consequence;
- public memory versus private truth;
- generational change;
- institutional outcome;
- transformed normality;
- a final perspective unavailable in the climax.

Do not use an epilogue to restate the ending.

## 8. Aftertaste design

Aftertaste can come from:

- unresolved but bounded uncertainty;
- ordinary continuity carrying visible residue;
- irony between what happened and what people believe happened;
- a relationship that changed without becoming simple;
- a final image whose meaning has accumulated across the work;
- recognition that the world continues outside the text.

Do not force profundity. Concrete altered life often carries more aftertaste than abstract summary.

## Audit

- Which closure axes are intentionally closed?
- Which remain open, and why?
- What is the last genuine state change?
- Is the ending landing once or repeatedly?
- Would one fewer explanatory sentence improve it?
- Does the ending leave consequences rather than reset?
- Does uncertainty feel meaningful or merely withheld?
- What does ordinary life look like after the story?

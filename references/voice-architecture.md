# Voice Architecture

> Voice is a selectable/formal project resource. Read current scoped `project-poetics.md` before treating stability, transparency, lyricism, restraint, or heterogeneity as desirable.

Use this reference before drafting Chapter 1 and whenever the prose begins to feel generic, over-polished, or stylistically unstable. Use `focalization-narrator.md` for information-camera discipline and `dialogue-character-voice.md` for speech behavior, subtext, register, and audience-dependent voice. For long-run numerical drift indicators, use `story-observatory.md` / `scripts/voice_drift.py`. A voice shift may be labeled authorized only when `voice_drift.py` receives a valid scoped Project Poetics derivative plus provenance envelope; a naked `--authorized-shift` assertion is insufficient; metrics may trigger review but never prove a voice error.

## 1. Build a project-specific voice

A project may have one stable baseline, several scope-specific regimes, or deliberate formal heterogeneity. Define voice at the smallest scope that remains useful. Do not reduce a project's poetics to a style preset.

Do not define voice only by prohibitions. Establish positive defaults for this novel.

Choose deliberately:

- narrative distance;
- viewpoint closeness;
- interiority density;
- narrator visibility;
- degree of irony;
- sentence elasticity;
- dialogue naturalism;
- descriptive density;
- descriptive temperament: how readily the narration stops to look, and how long it stays;
- literal-to-figurative balance;
- preferred sensory channels;
- tolerance for abstraction;
- humor source;
- metaphor source domain;
- treatment of violence;
- treatment of silence;
- degree of explicit emotional naming.

The goal is consistency with variation, not a rigid formula. Voice includes what the narration chooses not to say: some projects tolerate sharp ellipsis and direct interruption, while others need more connective tissue. Do not confuse smoothness with quality.

## 2. Socially grounded language

Characters should not share one authorial voice.

Let speech and thought vary with:

- class;
- education;
- profession;
- region;
- generation;
- institutional role;
- intimacy;
- fear;
- public versus private setting.

Avoid caricature accents unless the project genuinely needs them.

## 3. Literary influence without imitation

When the user cites literary authors as tonal references, translate the request into general qualities instead of copying distinctive phrasing.

Possible transferable qualities include:

- vulnerability and self-consciousness;
- dry self-irony;
- quiet attention to sensory detail;
- seasonality and transience;
- plain treatment of pain and absurdity;
- humane observation of manners and embarrassment;
- tension between private feeling and public role;
- ordinary objects accumulating emotional history;
- restraint around grief;
- comedy that emerges from social reality rather than punch lines.

Select only qualities that fit the world, viewpoint, culture, class, era, and scene.

## 4. Motif discipline

Choose a small motif palette only if the story naturally supports it.

Possible motif sources:

- local weather;
- infrastructure;
- food;
- clothing wear;
- bureaucratic objects;
- industrial sounds;
- religious or civic ritual;
- recurring routes;
- seasonal light;
- tools of a profession.

A motif should gain meaning through recurrence. Do not announce symbolism.

## 5. Concrete before abstract

Do not confuse concreteness with a single decorative image. If a place or object deserves attention, let literal shape, material, surface, light, position, use, wear, or movement exist before metaphor. See `environment-description.md` for the full descriptive protocol.


When possible, ground emotion or theme in:

- a task;
- a delay;
- a physical object;
- social embarrassment;
- money;
- bodily limitation;
- timing;
- habit;
- refusal;
- maintenance work;
- what someone chooses not to say.

Use abstraction when the viewpoint character would genuinely think abstractly.

## 6. Viewpoint fingerprints

For each recurring viewpoint, define differences deeper than catchphrases. Track: 

- what they notice first in a room;
- what they treat as normal and therefore omit;
- what they measure: danger, status, cost, beauty, procedure, bodily sensation, opportunity, embarrassment, etc.;
- what kinds of uncertainty bother them;
- whether thought tends toward concrete detail, abstraction, memory, planning, or social reading;
- what happens to syntax under stress;
- humor source and tolerance;
- what they refuse to name directly;
- what kinds of metaphors could plausibly arise from their life.

Two viewpoint passages should not become distinguishable only after names and pronouns are restored.

For sustained subjective narration, also retain what the voice repeatedly misclassifies, how it explains failure, excuses others or accuses itself, what it is ashamed to name, and how it moves between narrating-present and remembered experience. Carry these selected commitments into the scoped Writer derivative. Age, stress, relationship and retrospect may change the pattern; continuity is not a catchphrase prison.

Run a blind-voice check when characters start sounding alike: remove names from short internal or dialogue excerpts and ask whether identity remains inferable from attention, judgment, register, and rhythm. Do not exaggerate difference into caricature.

## 7. Voice drift versus authorized variation

For long projects, maintain baseline samples only as diagnostics. `scripts/voice_drift.py` is advisory.

Classify a change before correcting it:

1. **authorized scoped variation** - Project/arc/chapter/scene poetics explicitly allows the shift;
2. **character/register development** - audience, hierarchy, stress, fatigue, public/private context, age, or relation explains the shift;
3. **emergent poetics hypothesis** - repeated effective change deserves review through `discovery-drafting.md`;
4. **unauthorized drift** - no project/character/form reason supports the change.

Only category 4 is presumptively a repair target. Consistency may consist in a stable rule of transformation rather than surface sameness.

During revision ask whether a proposed normalization would erase constitutive form, not merely whether statistics differ.

## Core principle

A scene is a **locally coherent unit of sustained narrative attention**. It is not defined by event count and is not required to produce a durable world-state change.

A scene may earn its duration through:

- state transition or decision;
- experiential completion;
- relationship inhabitation;
- perceptual or atmospheric encounter;
- ritual/repetition/recurrence;
- waiting or deliberate stasis;
- memory/temporal return;
- formal or linguistic event;
- another contribution authorized by Project Poetics.

Chapters organize one or more scene/summary/ellipsis/pause contributions into an intelligible local experience. Arcs/volumes may inherit changed state, accumulated relation/familiarity, recurring structures, memory, social drift, or formal development. Do not force a twist or delta at every level.

## 1. Scene as a narrative-attention unit

Readers often segment narrative when goals, time, location, participants, causal situation, or object of attention changes. Use those cues when useful, but do not treat event-boundary research as a universal scene ontology.

Privately know the scene's **entry condition, attention contract, and intended contribution**.

### Entry

- time/place only at the precision causally needed;
- focalization and current attention owner;
- relevant body/resources/relations/knowledge;
- current Project Poetics / chapter override;
- why this moment deserves duration under active salience sources;
- what the reader is being asked to do: infer, notice, inhabit, wait, remember, judge, track form, etc.

### Process

Possible processes include action, resistance, conversation, evidence, refusal, misunderstanding, verification, bodily work, social maintenance, observation, memory, recurrence, waiting, formal modulation, or quiet continuation.

### Exit / completion

A scene may leave:

- a durable state delta;
- a reader-model update;
- a relationship/familiarity increment;
- a completed perceptual/atmospheric encounter;
- a reinforced or broken recurrence;
- a temporal or formal shift;
- a deliberate non-resolution or stasis that the reader has actually experienced;
- no durable world delta at all.

Do not invent a change merely to populate a delta field.

Local contribution vocabulary can include event, epistemic, social, relational, self-model, perceptual, obsessive, memory, interpretive/metaphysical and formal turns. These are composable descriptions, not mandatory beats or a hard enum. State the particular before/after or accumulation authorized by scoped Project Poetics; a scene may have one contribution or none of these traditional turns. Productive quiet changes attention, deepens familiarity, activates memory or makes a pattern legible. Inert repetition repeats perception, relation, pressure and interpretation without new accumulation or licensed experience. Conventional goal/obstacle/confrontation/victory is required only where the project actually uses it.

## 2. Entry contract

Before drafting, answer only what matters:

- Why is this moment worth page time rather than summary or ellipsis?
- What active pressure, relationship, uncertainty, need, or sensory/material condition makes the present state unstable or newly meaningful?
- What local question is the scene carrying?
- What would count as a meaningful change?

Do not begin every scene with arrival, greeting, seating, recap, and room inventory. Keep those steps only when they carry status, ritual, inconvenience, class, danger, or causal information.

If the scene materially depends on a real place, object, animal, custom, profession, institution, law, historical condition, science, or technology, verify the applicable dossier before locking the scene. Do not solve missing research with plausible-sounding invention. Use `reality-grounding-research.md`.

For what deserves page time at all, use `narrative-selection.md`. For time resolution, use `narrative-time-duration.md`.

## 3. Scene Packet handoff

Use a Scene Packet when time/place/access/custody/geometry continuity is causally material or when the project governance explicitly requires one. `spatiotemporal-scene-state.md` owns packet structure and synchronization.

A Packet protects mechanical continuity; it does **not** prove that a scene deserves page time.

This module owns the narrative-attention contract: why the scene deserves duration, which salience/progression mode is active, and what would count as completion. `no durable world delta` is a valid completion when Project Poetics authorizes experiential, relational, perceptual, recurrent, temporal, or formal work.

Do not let an optional Packet become a universal literary gate.

## 4. Desire, resistance, and tactics

In active conflict scenes, avoid repeating one tactic at higher volume.

Let participants adapt through moves such as:

- ask;
- flatter;
- delay;
- reframe;
- threaten;
- offer proof;
- expose cost;
- recruit an ally;
- change audience;
- invoke procedure;
- withdraw;
- lie;
- tell a partial truth;
- force a commitment;
- change the practical problem.

A scene feels intelligent when resistance changes method.

Do not require perfect optimization. Tactics should remain credible from the actor's information, habits, emotion, status, and risk tolerance.

## 5. Information scenes

Exposition is not a scene turn by itself.

When information enters a scene, ask what it does:

- changes a decision;
- changes trust;
- creates risk;
- removes an option;
- exposes a lie;
- forces verification;
- changes leverage;
- alters reader interpretation;
- creates an obligation or institutional consequence.

If people merely exchange facts and leave unchanged, summarize, compress, or redesign.

Use `dialogue-character-voice.md` when the information must arrive through socially distinct dialogue, and `knowledge-topology.md` when source, evidence, or reveal fairness is the real issue.

## 6. Quiet scenes

Do not punish quiet scenes for lacking external conflict.

They can justify page time through:

- emotional processing;
- intimacy or distance;
- changed self-understanding;
- ordinary-life baseline;
- recovery;
- social texture;
- anticipation;
- irreversible small decisions;
- new interpretation of a place, person, or past event.

A quiet scene may create a delta, deepen a durable baseline, let a relationship/routine/place be inhabited, complete a perceptual encounter, stage waiting or recurrence, or accomplish another Project-Poetics contribution. Its value does not depend on later disturbance.

Do not force a clue, argument, twist, or theme speech into every quiet scene.

## 7. Reaction and decision units

After a major disruption, allow enough processing for the next choice to feel owned by the character.

A reaction unit may move through:

- immediate bodily or emotional response;
- interpretation;
- competing costs;
- decision;
- new objective.

Do not require this sequence after every scene. Compress or omit it when obvious, repetitive, or better shown through the next action.

For earned psychological change, use `character-dynamics.md` and `state-inertia.md`.

## 8. Scene boundaries and length

Enter late when setup is inferable. Leave when the meaningful local change has landed.

Do not automatically include:

- greetings;
- full travel;
- every explanation;
- exit logistics;
- post-event paraphrase of what the reader already understood.

Scene length should respond to:

- decision complexity;
- emotional difficulty;
- tactical reversals;
- verification burden;
- spatial/sensory importance;
- aftermath weight;
- reader orientation needs.

Do not give every scene the same beat count or reversal count.

Use `restraint-negative-space.md` when the scene keeps explaining after the landing.


### Scene boundaries are not paragraph boundaries

Do not map each scene beat to a paragraph mechanically. A paragraph may contain several micro-beats under one dominant local state, while one major beat may require a paragraph cluster. Use `paragraph-architecture.md` for the sentence-to-paragraph-to-cluster layer.

## 9. Causal bridges and environmental continuity

When moving from A to C, make sure B exists in the world.

B may be travel, preparation, recovery, paperwork, training, transmission, investigation, negotiation, emotional processing, or resource acquisition.

B does not always deserve a scene. Choose scene, summary, ellipsis, reconstruction, or implication through `narrative-selection.md` and `narrative-time-duration.md`.

A scene should also not become an empty dialogue chamber after one establishing sentence. When place materially matters, establish enough physical field for movement and perception to occur, then let selected parts of that field continue while people act.

Use `environment-description.md` for descriptive resolution and spatial attention. Use `dynamic-vividness.md` for moving-world geometry, affordances, occlusion, independent processes, and aftermath.

## 10. Strategic scenes

Before negotiation, investigation, deception, or maneuvering, know:

- each side's objective;
- uncertainty and evidence;
- leverage and authority;
- outside options;
- time pressure;
- reputational or institutional constraints;
- verification channels;
- walk-away conditions.

For formal councils, boards, hearings, succession disputes, treaties, or procedure-heavy conflict, use `institutional-negotiation.md`.

For adversarial plausibility, use `adversarial-review.md` before locking the outcome.

## 11. Combat scenes

Know:

- terrain and mission objective;
- force composition;
- injuries and fatigue;
- capability costs and counters;
- sensory limits;
- command structure;
- civilians and infrastructure where relevant;
- retreat and surrender conditions;
- what each side knows about the other;
- emotional stakes established before the clash.

Keep each exchange tied to a changed tactical or physical state rather than a list of named abilities.

After combat, commit bodily, informational, material, social, political, and institutional consequences.

For kinetic rendering, use `dynamic-vividness.md`.

## 12. Chapter movement

A chapter may contain several scenes, but it should have intelligible local movement.

Examples:

- a question becomes sharper;
- a plan gains or loses feasibility;
- a relationship shifts;
- a cost arrives;
- baseline is disrupted;
- a clue changes interpretation;
- public status changes;
- an old consequence matures;
- private action becomes institutional pressure.

Do not manufacture a cliffhanger if ordinary closure plus unresolved larger pressure is stronger.

For chapter-to-chapter pulse and expectation, use `rhythm-propulsion.md`.

## 13. Arc inheritance

Track:

- initiating pressure;
- competing objectives;
- structural constraints;
- information/resource/relationship progression;
- irreversible threshold;
- climax conditions;
- aftermath;
- world changes inherited by later arcs.

An arc should change the conditions under which later arcs occur, not merely finish an event chain.

For multi-volume direction and thread inheritance, use `long-horizon-architecture.md`.

## 14. Pacing handoff

Pacing is not speed alone. It combines:

- information density;
- unresolved questions;
- distance between cause and consequence;
- anticipation versus action;
- recovery time;
- decision complexity;
- scene length;
- public versus private pressure.

This file owns unit structure, not the full pacing system.

Use:

- `narrative-time-duration.md` for scene/summary/ellipsis/pause;
- `rhythm-propulsion.md` for expectation, cuts, repetition, unequal weight, and thread pulse;
- `reader-model.md` for memory burden and question activation.

## 15. Scene existence and removal test

Before spending full scene-level page time, record a compact `existence_reason` in the Scene Construction Sheet: what reader experience, state transition, relationship pressure, perception change, irreversible observation, or dramatic embodiment requires this unit to exist as a scene rather than summary/ellipsis.

Then run the **Remove-Scene Test**:

> If this scene vanished and adjacent transitions were repaired minimally, what durable or experiential loss would remain?

Possible valid losses include:

- a state/knowledge/relationship delta that cannot be carried honestly elsewhere;
- an important choice whose lived pressure matters more than its result;
- a perceptual or emotional encounter the reader needs to experience directly;
- necessary spatial/material causality;
- a distinctive reversal, confrontation, discovery, or quiet reorientation;
- a thematic counterexample embodied through action rather than explanation.

If the honest answer is "almost nothing," prefer merge, compression, summary, or deletion. Do not keep a scene merely because effort has already been spent planning it.

## 16. Scene contribution audit

At scene exit, identify what changed in at least one of:

- plan;
- knowledge;
- belief;
- relationship;
- leverage;
- obligation;
- body;
- resource;
- status;
- location;
- reader interpretation;
- emotional baseline;
- durable lived baseline.

If nothing changed and no durable baseline deepened, ask why the scene is present.

### Tactic-repeat test

If the middle can be summarized as "they continue arguing/fighting/searching" without a change in method, evidence, power, route, or interpretation, adapt or compress.

### Conference-room test

If everyone begins and ends with the same knowledge, plan, relationships, leverage, and obligations, the meeting probably belongs in summary unless the lived social texture itself is the point.

### Feature-invisibility test

Do not plan scenes primarily as labels such as "politics scene," "daily-life scene," "foreshadowing scene," "literary scene," or "explanation scene."

Plan what people need to do, what constrains them, what they perceive, and what changes. Let politics, daily life, theme, setup, and literary texture emerge through truthful action.

# Rhythm and Narrative Propulsion

Use this reference when prose is causally sound but flat, uniformly weighted, over-bridged, over-explained, or mechanically patterned.

This module owns expectation movement, event weight, cuts, repetition/variation, local release/tightening, and cross-thread pulse. It does not own temporal resolution (`narrative-time-duration.md`) or explanation restraint (`restraint-negative-space.md`).

## Core principle

Rhythm is patterned movement in attention, expectation, duration, recurrence, pressure, interpretation, syntax, and silence. **Propulsion is only one possible rhythmic aim.**

Expectation movement can come from returning memory, status-sensitive speech, relationship distance, obsession, perception of place, competing hypotheses or self-recognition. Use the current progression commitment in `project-poetics.md`; a plot question is one possible carrier, not the default reader task.

Let page time follow the experience the current Project Poetics asks the reader to undergo. Fast change may be compressed; slow or unchanged conditions may be expanded when waiting, habit, recurrence, bodily duration, atmosphere, or formal pattern is itself the experience.

Do not infer literary value from pace alone.

## 1. Do not announce what can arrive by itself

Be suspicious of bridge language whose only job is announcing the next line:

- then;
- just then;
- suddenly;
- at that moment;
- the next instant;
- meanwhile;
- as soon as the words fell;
- the atmosphere changed;
- he realized something;
- everyone fell silent.

These are not banned. Keep them when orientation genuinely requires them. Remove them when the event itself already performs the transition.

## 2. Compress or expand by experiential function, not change magnitude alone

Compression is useful when an interval adds no project-authorized experience and only repeats already-settled information.

Expansion is justified when duration itself matters, including:

- consequential action or decision;
- relation maintenance or erosion;
- waiting, boredom, recovery, labor, travel, ritual, routine;
- perception/atmosphere/place becoming legible;
- repetition that establishes baseline or pattern;
- memory/association unfolding;
- formal/syntactic work whose temporal experience matters.

A long interval with little state change may deserve pages. A major state change may deserve one sentence. Diagnose against scoped Project Poetics before cutting.

## 3. Repetition creates beat; variation creates life; pattern-break creates impact

Useful repetition includes repeated refusal, test result, procedure, habit, combat probe, joke structure, or institutional routine.

Across repetitions, change at least one dimension such as:

- stakes;
- speaker;
- cost;
- interpretation;
- speed;
- audience;
- emotional meaning;
- practical consequence.

The break gains force because the reader felt the pattern first.

## 4. Use hard cuts only where inference is safe

Good omissions include routine travel, familiar preparation, repeated administrative steps, obvious interface appearance, and ordinary reactions whose exact mechanics do not matter.

Do not cut across:

- unclear motive;
- a critical causal step;
- an earned relationship transition;
- spatial information needed for action;
- evidence required for a fair reveal.

Make the reader perform safe inference, not repair authorial gaps.

## 5. Rhythm through question pressure

`reader-model.md` owns the reader-question stack. Rhythm uses changes in **pressure on that stack**: evidence can sharpen a question, consequence can replace it, delay can increase or decrease urgency, and a quieter thread can cool one question while warming another.

If the governing question persists too long without changed pressure, evidence, interpretation, or cost, reconfigure the sequence or compress. Do not manufacture arbitrary reversals only to keep visible motion.

## 6. Preserve unequal event weight

Do not give every event the same ceremony of setup -> announcement -> reaction -> explanation -> transition.

A minor visitor, joke, procedural update, betrayal, death, and worldview-breaking reveal should not all receive equal paragraph weight.

Major events may gain force through:

- a pause;
- fewer words rather than more;
- narrowed sensory focus;
- delayed reaction;
- changed syntax;
- conspicuous absence of commentary.

Importance does not require louder adjectives.


## 6A. Paragraph refresh frequency

Paragraph breaks change the reader's attention-refresh frequency. Rapid dialogue or unstable social control may legitimately produce short blocks; sustained reflection, summary, description, or argument may require longer blocks. Do not manipulate rhythm by arbitrary whitespace. Let semantic paragraphing come from `paragraph-architecture.md`, then judge the resulting cluster for pressure and variation here.

## 7. Dialogue timing matters as much as wording

Do not automatically answer every line immediately.

Possible timing moves include:

- delayed answer;
- answer through action;
- interruption;
- deliberate non-answer;
- topic shift;
- later recognition of a contradiction;
- formal language replacing expected intimacy.

Use timing only when character and situation support it. Do not turn hesitation into a trick used every page.

For the social action of dialogue itself, use `dialogue-character-voice.md`.

## 8. Vary information mode

Avoid one uniform mode of explanation.

Useful modes include:

- **drop** - small anomaly with incomplete meaning;
- **block** - clear fact that sharply updates the reader model;
- **blank** - no explanation yet because the current scene remains legible without it.

Blank space is not automatic mystery. The missing answer must be genuinely inaccessible or unnecessary now.

## 9. Quiet beats need not apologize for being quiet

A quiet beat should be intentional, specific, and situated, but it does not owe the reader a hidden hook, future payoff, micro-transformation, or symbolic assignment.

It may sustain:

- familiar routine;
- relation and common ground;
- embodied/material life;
- waiting or recovery;
- place/season;
- small perception;
- humor or social friction;
- simple continuity.

Use Project Poetics to decide whether the beat is doing necessary experiential work. Do not retrofit suspense simply to prove relevance.

## 10. Institutional rhythm

Political or procedural scenes often gain force from long calm interrupted by discrete changes in the option set:

- a record is requested;
- framing changes;
- jurisdiction is challenged;
- a motion becomes formal;
- a vote is called;
- a deep audit is proposed;
- a public commitment is forced.

After escalation, the room may become calm again. Institutions absorb shocks and continue operating.

Use `institutional-negotiation.md` for the underlying power mechanics.

## 11. Combat rhythm

Avoid sustained maximum intensity.

A fight may pulse through probe, contact, separation, assessment, pressure, failed assumption, adaptation, reset, irreversible cost, finish, or retreat.

Do not force a universal sequence. Let ability, terrain, injury, mission, information, and objective determine tempo.

A dangerous action may take fewer words than the setup that makes it dangerous.

## 12. Chapter-to-chapter modulation

Across several chapters, vary naturally among:

- public and private stakes;
- action and interpretation;
- social and solitary scenes;
- high and low information density;
- external pressure and aftermath;
- open conflict and quiet adjustment.

Do not alternate mechanically. Consequences should determine modulation.

## 13. Cross-thread pulse and dormancy

For multi-thread long fiction, do not rotate A/B/C plots on a visible schedule merely to prove they still exist.

Useful pulse states include:

- **active** - producing immediate scenes or consequences;
- **latent** - progressing offscreen with occasional surface traces;
- **dormant** - temporarily quiet but still causally alive;
- **reactivating** - returning because a condition changed;
- **converging** - beginning to affect another thread;
- **resolving** - paying off while leaving residue.

Stagger these states. Several threads may remain alive while only one deserves page time.

A dormant thread must still have state. Before reactivation, update what happened during silence rather than restoring it exactly where the reader last saw it.

Use `long-horizon-architecture.md` for arc inheritance and `reader-model.md` for memory decay during dormancy.

## 14. Rhythm audit

### Bridge deletion

Underline sentences whose only function is announcing the next event. Delete provisionally; restore only what orientation requires.

### Equal-weight test

Mark events minor / medium / major. Check whether prose length and reaction ceremony accidentally flatten them.

### Expectation map

Write the reader's likely question for each segment. If it does not move for too long, compress or deepen pressure.

### Repetition map

Find repeated actions or results. Ask whether they establish pattern, vary it, break it, or merely repeat information.

### Safe-inference test

Identify intermediate steps the reader can reconstruct. Remove some without breaking causality.

### Pulse test

Locate where attention tightens, releases, pauses, and changes direction. If every paragraph carries similar pressure, redesign before sentence-level polish. If paragraph lengths are mechanically alternating or uniformly fragmented, audit the semantic boundaries in `paragraph-architecture.md`.

For explanation echoes and stop-earlier decisions, use `restraint-negative-space.md` rather than duplicating that audit here.

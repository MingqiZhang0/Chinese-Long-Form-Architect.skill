# Restraint, Negative Space, and Page Density

> SELECTABLE POETICS CAPABILITY. Restraint and omission are powerful, not universally superior. Do not use this module to make every project quieter, terser, or less explicit than its Project Poetics requires.

Use this reference when prose over-explains, every detail carries symbolic or plot weight, endings keep talking after they land, or the page feels uniformly dense.

## Core principle

Meaning is created by the relation between what is shown and what the reader is allowed to complete.

Restraint is not vagueness. Omission works when enough structure exists for the missing part to acquire shape.

## 1. Distinguish useful gaps from missing craft

Useful gap:

- the viewpoint lacks access;
- the reader has enough evidence to infer a range of possibilities;
- the omitted explanation is not needed to understand current action;
- the absence itself creates pressure, implication, or social realism.

Bad gap:

- hides the only causal explanation;
- withholds what the POV is consciously thinking solely to trick the reader;
- removes orientation or motive required for fairness;
- substitutes obscurity for depth.

## 2. Explanation-echo cut

After a strong action, image, silence, or line of dialogue, check whether the next sentence merely translates it.

If the reader can already infer the meaning, cut or redirect the sentence.

Common echo pattern:

> behavior -> narrator explains behavior -> internal thought repeats explanation -> another character names it

Usually one or two layers are enough.

## 3. Single-landing rule

Do not make the same emotional or thematic point several times in succession.

When a beat lands:

- let action continue;
- allow silence;
- change scale;
- cut the scene;
- move to consequence.

Use `endings-closure-aftertaste.md` at major endpoints.

## 4. Density modulation

Not every paragraph should carry equal amounts of:

- clue;
- symbolism;
- emotion;
- worldbuilding;
- wit;
- theme;
- plot turn.

Allow lower-density passages where people work, wait, move, maintain, recover, misunderstand, or simply inhabit the world.

High density becomes more legible when surrounded by ordinary texture.

## 5. World surplus

Do not require every object, side character, routine, rumor, image, or historical fact to pay off the central plot.

Some details justify themselves by making the world inhabitable, revealing class or routine, or belonging to someone else's life.

Surplus should still fit the world. It simply does not owe the protagonist a payoff.

## 6. Motif restraint

A motif gains force through selective recurrence and changed context.

Watch for motif monoculture:

- title;
- place name;
- imagery;
- dialogue;
- powers;
- clues;
- climax;
- ending

all literalizing the same concept.

If the world seems to know what the story is about, loosen one link.

## 7. Trust-the-reader test

Ask:

- Has the evidence already done the work?
- Is the next sentence useful for action, rhythm, voice, or new meaning?
- Am I explaining because the reader needs it, or because the author fears silence?
- Does the ambiguity have boundaries?

## 8. Stop-one-sentence-earlier test

At scene, chapter, and ending boundaries, temporarily remove the final sentence.

If the remaining version is clearer, stronger, stranger, sadder, or more confident, keep it cut.

Do not use this as a ritual. Sometimes the final sentence contains the actual state change.

## 9. Anti-patterns

- every ordinary object becomes foreshadowing;
- every silence is labeled meaningful;
- every image receives symbolic interpretation;
- every scene ends with a thesis;
- every mystery answer is immediately restated;
- every emotional beat gets one more confirming reaction;
- minimalism that withholds basic causal or spatial information.

## Audit

- What can the reader already complete?
- Which sentence is translating an image that needs no translation?
- Is page density varying?
- Are there things in the world that matter locally but not thematically?
- Is a motif recurring because it changed meaning, or because the author likes it?
- Could the scene end earlier without losing the state change?

## 1. Model the causal cone

World-model breadth is not literary prestige. Model only the systems needed by hard causality or the current Project Poetics. A project may intentionally use a thin world, a highly focalized/subjective world, a fairy-tale explanatory threshold, or a dense autonomous social ecology.

Do not build the entire universe at equal depth.

**Activation sentinel:** if the opening or near causal chain depends on how institutions, urban/rural space, ordinary livelihood, law, information channels, social hierarchy, logistics, or offscreen autonomous actors constrain what can happen, World Model is not optional merely because those facts could be improvised scene-by-scene. Record the activation decision in the prewriting readiness record.

Deeply model what near-term characters can affect or be affected by:

- relevant places and routes;
- active institutions;
- current economy and scarcity;
- applicable law and jurisdiction;
- information channels;
- current power or technology rules;
- social expectations;
- historical residue that still constrains behavior.

Keep distant regions, future institutions, and irrelevant trivia open until the story approaches them.

## 2. Ontology and generative rules

For every extraordinary element answer:

- What exists?
- What can interact with what?
- What is conserved, consumed, transferred, or transformed?
- What can reliably detect it?
- What cannot happen?
- What are the dominant edge cases?
- Which rules are natural laws and which are social conventions?
- What apparent exceptions can be explained by deeper rules?

Prefer a small number of generative principles over many unrelated exceptions.

## 3. Progression / persistence model (conditional)

Represent progression through multiple dimensions when useful:

- raw capacity;
- control precision;
- knowledge;
- endurance and recovery;
- technique access;
- equipment;
- authority;
- infrastructure;
- network;
- legitimacy;
- risk tolerance or corruption exposure.

For each qualitative tier define:

- entry condition;
- newly possible actions;
- remaining impossibilities;
- cost and time;
- failure modes;
- countermeasures;
- societal role;
- population frequency;
- institutional recruitment or regulation;
- new vulnerabilities created by advancement.

Avoid pure numeric inflation when qualitative change creates richer choices.

## 4. Geography, transport, and logistics

Locations should exist for reasons.

For important places consider:

- terrain and climate;
- water and food;
- trade access;
- defensive value;
- transport network;
- dominant industry;
- class geography;
- administrative boundaries;
- migration;
- policing;
- seasonal rhythms;
- sensory identity;
- travel time and chokepoints.

Geography should constrain strategy, economy, politics, and ordinary life.

## 5. Institutions and power

For each major institution record:

- legal or public mandate;
- actual incentives;
- budget and resource source;
- leadership selection;
- promotion and punishment;
- coercive capacity;
- information access;
- legitimacy base;
- dependencies;
- internal factions;
- procedural chokepoints;
- exploitable processes;
- historical grievance;
- collapse, split, or reform conditions.

Map mechanisms of power, not titles alone.

Operational bodily limits, finite inventories, throughput, capacity, resupply, recovery, and scene-to-scene resource conservation are owned by `embodied-resource-state.md`; this file owns the macro systems that generate those constraints.

## 6. Economy and scarcity

Track what materially sustains the world:

- food and water;
- energy;
- housing;
- transport;
- communications;
- medicine;
- power-system resources;
- labor;
- education and credentialing;
- money and credit;
- taxation;
- storage;
- trade;
- insurance or risk-sharing where relevant;
- black markets;
- critical bottlenecks.

For any rare resource ask who produces, transports, protects, prices, taxes, steals, counterfeits, and substitutes for it.

## 7. Information ecology

Define:

- communication speed;
- authentication;
- archives and records;
- censorship;
- surveillance;
- propaganda;
- literacy and technical access;
- rumor channels;
- institutional compartmentalization;
- costs of verification;
- realistic secret half-life.

Strategic fiction depends on credible information asymmetry.



### Observational side effects and information powers

If the world contains prophecy, prediction, divination, mind reading, remote viewing, retrocognition, magical sensing, or other privileged information channels, model them as world mechanisms rather than narrative permissions.

Define:

- query scope and precision;
- temporal or spatial range;
- cost and recovery;
- uncertainty and branching;
- target complexity;
- detectability or trace;
- whether observation changes the observed system;
- repetition signatures;
- self-reference behavior;
- known counters and false-input attacks;
- cultural, professional, legal, religious, or institutional responses.

Distinguish the actual mechanism from folk or professional explanations of it. Several cultures or institutions may describe the same phenomenon differently.

For complex strategic use, load `references/hidden-causal-networks.md`.

## 8. Law and procedure

Know:

- jurisdiction;
- investigation powers;
- evidence rules where relevant;
- detention and punishment;
- licensing;
- property and inheritance;
- emergency powers;
- institutional immunity;
- appeal or review;
- treatment of extraordinary abilities;
- procedural delay.

Bureaucracy is not filler. Procedure creates leverage, delay, evidence, and legitimacy.

## 9. History with residue

For recurring places, long time spans, generational turnover, informal networks, rumor, and autonomous local change, extend this section with `world-evolution-social-ecology.md`. History should continue accumulating after the protagonist leaves.


Create history when it leaves present consequences:

- borders;
- infrastructure;
- legal exceptions;
- inherited privilege;
- demographic scars;
- displaced communities;
- language and names;
- doctrine;
- architecture;
- ritual;
- property ownership;
- debt;
- institutional distrust;
- public myths that distort memory.

History without residue is usually trivia.

## 10. Ordinary life model

Know without necessarily narrating directly:

- common housing;
- household composition;
- meals;
- work and school schedules;
- commuting;
- healthcare;
- wages and recurring expenses;
- marriage and funerals;
- childcare and elder care;
- entertainment;
- shopping;
- sanitation;
- weather adaptation;
- bureaucratic chores;
- neighborhood relations;
- status markers;
- common complaints.

Different class, profession, age, region, and era should produce different daily baselines.

## 11. Cultural and social norms

Model:

- politeness and insult;
- obligation and reciprocity;
- shame and prestige;
- hospitality;
- privacy;
- gender or family expectations where relevant;
- taboo;
- religion or ritual where relevant;
- dress;
- slang;
- public versus private behavior.

Norms should create both comfort and constraint.

## 12. Long-form tension map

Prefer tensions that can generate many different stories:

- center versus periphery;
- old elite versus rising class;
- law versus practical control;
- transparency versus secrecy;
- scarcity versus legitimacy;
- innovation versus incumbent power;
- institutional mission versus self-preservation;
- public duty versus private loyalty;
- generational change;
- demographic pressure;
- historical grievance;
- uneven access to power.

Reasonable people should be able to collide without one side becoming irrational.

## 13. Narrative affordance map

During architecture, note a small number of usable possibilities for each important subsystem:

- procedures that can be delayed, appealed, audited, forged, or weaponized;
- resources that create leverage or dependency;
- routes that can trap, separate, protect, or expose;
- customs that conceal intent or create obligation;
- rules with honest edge cases;
- institutional conflicts where both sides can act rationally;
- records, routines, or objects that could later support evidence;
- vulnerabilities that competent actors will eventually counter;
- historical residue that can become politically or emotionally active.

Classify affordances as:

- available now;
- provisional;
- locked by prose;
- retired before promise.

Do not force all affordances into the plot.

## 14. Setup opportunities during architecture

For likely future pressure points ask:

- What ordinary fact would exist now because the world requires it?
- Could it later become causally relevant without changing its original reason to exist?
- Would the detail still make sense if the planned payoff changed?
- Can a major future turn have several independent supports?

Prefer setups with honest first-use value.

## 15. Theme as structural question

When Project Poetics specifically activates thematic polyphony, use `thematic-polyphony.md` to distribute competing positions across characters, institutions, and consequences. Other projects may realize theme through perception, action, memory, relation, form, or remain thematically open.


Do not write a thesis statement for the novel.

Instead identify a small set of recurring questions the world can force different people to answer through action.

Examples of question shapes:

- What does power require a person to become?
- When does institutional loyalty conflict with private loyalty?
- Who gets to define legitimate violence?
- What does survival cost when scarcity is unequal?

Let institutions and characters embody conflicting answers. Do not make the narrator deliver a final slogan.

## 16. Readiness boundary

The world model is ready for prose when:

- the current causal cone has no known fatal contradiction;
- active rules have costs and counters;
- institutions have incentives;
- travel, communication, and resource constraints are plausible;
- major planned actions have legal, economic, and social consequences;
- ordinary life has enough material specificity to feel inhabited;
- future conflict can grow from existing tensions;
- remaining unknowns are controlled open space rather than hidden holes.

Stop modeling when additional work mostly adds remote trivia.

# Chinese Grammar, Idiom, and Naturalness Standard

Use this reference when drafting or revising **modern Chinese prose or dialogue** and the main risk is grammatical naturalness, reference clarity, aspect/negation, discourse particles, connective load, `把/被` structure, `的/地/得`, idiom use, or register fit. It owns Chinese-specific sentence and phraseological naturalness. `prose-standard.md` owns final prose integration; `dialogue-character-voice.md` owns character speech strategy and idiolect; `voice-architecture.md` owns project-level register.

This module is **not** a school-grammar checker and must not flatten literary Chinese into textbook prose. Treat grammar as a system of meaning and information flow. Deliberate ellipsis, dialect, internet speech, archaizing syntax, fragmentary consciousness, and character-specific nonstandard language are allowed when the effect is intelligible, controlled, and consistent with the project voice.

## 1. Operating principle

When Chinese prose sounds unnatural, identify **what relation the grammar is encoding** before replacing words.

Prefer this order:

1. recover the intended event, participant, time/aspect, information structure, and stance;
2. decide which participant/topic is currently accessible;
3. choose the Chinese construction that expresses those relations naturally;
4. preserve the project's register and character voice;
5. only then tune rhythm.

Do not repair Chinese by mechanically deleting subjects, adding particles, swapping `不` for `没有`, inserting four-character phrases, or converting every active clause into `把/被`.

Distinguish three levels:

- **semantic/grammatical defect** — changes or obscures who did what, whether it happened, temporal/aspectual status, reference, or idiom meaning;
- **probable naturalness problem** — grammatically interpretable but translation-like, over-explicit, register-mismatched, or poorly scoped;
- **stylistic option** — several forms are grammatical and the project voice decides among them.

Only the first level should normally behave like a hard correction.

## 2. Reference and topic continuity

Modern Chinese readily uses **zero anaphora** and topic chains when the referent is highly accessible. Repeating a full noun or third-person pronoun in every clause can produce translated or mechanically explicit prose.

Use zero reference when:

- the same discourse topic continues;
- there is no plausible competing antecedent;
- action order and paragraph structure keep the actor clear;
- omission improves flow without forcing rereading.

Restore an explicit noun/name/pronoun when:

- another same-gender or same-category referent has entered the local context;
- the topic changes;
- a paragraph or scene re-enters after intervening material;
- viewpoint, time, location, or action ownership shifts;
- a zero subject would make two readings genuinely plausible.

Example principle:

```text
小周把门推开，先看了一眼走廊，又退回来关了灯。
```

Do not expand this automatically into repeated `他` merely for completeness.

But when two men are active:

```text
小周把门推开。老陈从楼梯口走上来。小周退回屋里，关了灯。
```

The repeated name is useful because the discourse now has a competitor.

Treat paragraph boundaries as partial accessibility resets, not absolute resets. A stable scene topic can continue across paragraphs; after a long interruption, explicit re-entry is often cheaper than making the reader solve a pronoun puzzle.

Never optimize for the fewest pronouns. Optimize for **minimum explicitness consistent with immediate reference clarity**.

## 3. Negation: `不` versus `没/没有`

Do not treat `不` and `没/没有` as stylistic variants.

A useful working distinction is:

- `没/没有` normally negates the **realization, occurrence, completion, possession, or existence** of an event/state in the relevant situation;
- `不` commonly negates **habit, property, willingness, decision, future/conditional action, generic judgment**, or another non-realized/modal relation.

For bounded scene events that simply did not happen, normally prefer `没/没有 + V`:

- `他看着她，没有解释。`
- `她听完以后没回答。`
- `门没有关严。`

Do not compress the same eventive meaning into a bare comma-tail for synthetic hardness:

- avoid as default: `他看着她，不解释。`
- avoid as default: `她听完了，不回答。`

Keep `不 + V` when its semantics are real:

- habit/property: `他一向不解释这些事。`
- refusal/volition: `他决定不解释。`
- future/condition: `现在不走，就赶不上末班车。`
- generic rule: `这种门不向外开。`

Natural `没有/没` is not weak prose. Do not delete it merely to imitate clipped literary rhythm.

Corrective negative frames such as `不是 X，而是 Y`, `并非 X，而是 Y`, `没有 X，只是 Y` are legitimate when the contrast itself matters. Reduce them when they become a habitual emphasis template.

## 4. Aspect and temporal anchoring: `了 / 着 / 过`

Do not map English tense mechanically onto Chinese particles. Chinese temporal interpretation depends on time expressions, aspect, event structure, discourse order, and context; `了 / 着 / 过` are not simple past-tense endings.

Working distinctions:

- `了` often marks completion/perfective viewpoint or a new situation/change of state, depending on position and construction;
- `着` commonly presents a continuing state or ongoing configuration;
- `过` commonly presents experiential occurrence relative to a reference point.

Revision questions:

- Is the sentence marking **completion**, **continuation**, **experience**, or merely **past time**?
- Is a temporal adverb or narrative sequence already sufficient?
- Has `了` been copied onto every past action because the source model is thinking in tense?
- Does deleting the particle change aspect or discourse status rather than merely shortening the sentence?

Avoid chains in which every completed action carries an unnecessary `了`:

```text
他打开了门，走了进去，拿了杯子，倒了水。
```

A natural revision may distribute aspect according to discourse focus rather than mark every verb identically:

```text
他打开门走进去，拿起杯子倒了水。
```

This is not a global deletion rule. Keep `了` where completion/change is contrastive or structurally required.

Likewise, do not remove `着` from genuine sustained configurations merely because repeated particles look inelegant. Grammar outranks cosmetic variety.

## 5. Result, direction, `把`, and `被`

Chinese frequently packages event boundaries through **result and directional complements**. When the narrative fact is a changed state, prefer a construction that makes the result legible instead of translating an English-style manner-heavy clause word for word.

Examples of result/direction information include:

- `推开 / 关上 / 撞倒 / 擦干 / 找到 / 看清 / 走进去 / 拿出来`.

Use `把` when the clause genuinely profiles the disposal/handling of a sufficiently identifiable object and its affected result, location, quantity, or state is relevant:

```text
他把湿外套搭在椅背上。
```

Do not force `把` into every transitive action merely to sound Chinese.

Use `被` when the affected participant, passive relation, or loss of control deserves discourse prominence. Do not mechanically mirror English passive voice. A Chinese active, subjectless, `让/叫/给`, or lexical construction may be more natural depending on register and viewpoint.

When converting among SVO, `把`, and `被`, re-evaluate information focus and affectedness. They are not lossless stylistic permutations.

## 6. `的 / 地 / 得`

For standard modern written narration, distinguish the three structural particles by function:

- `的` — primarily marks attributive/nominal modification or nominalization;
- `地` — marks an adverbial modifier before the predicate where the construction calls for it;
- `得` — follows a verb/adjective and introduces a complement of degree, state, result, or possibility in the relevant construction.

Examples:

- `潮湿的墙面`
- `慢慢地转过身`
- `跑得太快`

Do not reduce this to a phonetic rule: spoken `de` is homophonous, but written choices carry structural information.

Also do not insert `地` after every adjective/adverbial. Many common adverbial structures naturally omit it. Judge the construction and rhythm, not a school mnemonic.

Character-authored notes, chat logs, dialect writing, child writing, or intentionally nonstandard documents may deviate when that deviation belongs to characterization. Narratorial standard prose should not inherit accidental errors from those embedded registers.

## 7. Connectives and clause linking

Chinese can express clause relations through word order, topic continuity, aspect, lexical semantics, and context without marking every logical edge with an explicit connective.

Use connectives such as `但是 / 可是 / 不过 / 然而 / 因此 / 所以 / 于是 / 然后 / 因而 / 总之` when they contribute real scope, discourse organization, stance, or contrast.

Do not produce a translated chain in which every sentence announces its logical relation:

```text
因此……但是……于是……然而……所以……
```

Before adding a connective, ask:

- Would the relation remain immediately clear from event order or semantics?
- Does the connective operate inside one sentence, across sentences, or across a larger discourse segment?
- Does its register fit narration or this speaker?
- Are several nearby sentences using the same global connector because the model is over-explaining coherence?

Parataxis is not permission for vagueness. If omission creates causal or temporal ambiguity, state the relation.

## 8. Sentence-final particles and discourse markers

`啊/呀`, `吧`, `呢`, `嘛`, sentence-final `了`, and conversational markers such as `你看`, `你知道`, `就是说`, `怎么说呢` carry stance, turn-management, shared-knowledge assumptions, relevance, uncertainty, insistence, continuation, contrast, and interpersonal positioning.

Do not sprinkle particles into dialogue merely to make it "more Chinese" or "more spoken".

For each recurring speaker, track:

- certainty versus tentativeness;
- power relation;
- age/social register where relevant;
- whether the particle seeks response, softens a directive, marks obviousness, continues a topic, or closes it;
- habitual discourse markers and whether they intensify under stress.

Two characters with identical particle patterns often sound model-generated even when every sentence is individually grammatical.

Do not strip all particles in pursuit of "clean prose" either. Removing them can erase interpersonal meaning.

## 9. Punctuation and syntactic responsibility

Use the current standard Chinese punctuation rules as the default baseline. At the time of this Skill revision (2026-10), `GB/T 15834-2011 标点符号用法` remains the current national standard; re-check freshness when a later publishing-standard decision materially depends on it. Literary deviation may be deliberate, but punctuation should not be asked to repair a broken clause.

A comma can mark rhythm, clause relation, or discourse grouping. It cannot by itself establish a missing grammatical owner.

This is why:

```text
他看着她，不解释。
```

may remain ambiguous or telegraphic when the intended meaning is merely a bounded non-occurrence. Changing the comma does not solve the underlying negation/ownership problem.

Similarly, do not use em dashes, ellipses, or repeated full stops as universal substitutes for syntax and information structure.

When punctuation is highly stylized, verify that a plain paraphrase of the sentence still has recoverable participants, event relation, and temporal logic.

## 10. Idiom and fixed-expression protocol

Treat a `成语` as a **lexicalized fixed expression with conventionalized meaning and grammatical behavior**, not as a four-character ornament.

Before using an uncertain idiom, verify five things:

### 10.1 Meaning

Do not infer meaning from the four surface characters. Many idioms are non-compositional or have conventional meanings narrower than their literal image.

If lookup is available, prefer a normative dictionary or corpus-backed reference such as `现代汉语词典` or `新华成语词典` rather than guessing from literal form.

### 10.2 Syntactic slot

Idioms can function as predicates, attributes, adverbials, complements, subjects, objects, or parenthetical material depending on the idiom. They are not a single grammatical class.

Check whether the specific idiom naturally occupies the slot you are giving it.

Do not reason:

> "The meaning fits, therefore the syntax fits."

### 10.3 Collocational target and semantic role

Check **who or what the idiom conventionally describes** and what roles its internal meaning presupposes.

A semantically adjacent idiom may still be wrong because it selects a different human/nonhuman target, action type, evaluative frame, degree, direction, or discourse situation.

### 10.4 Evaluative and pragmatic color

Check praise/blame, irony, humility/respect, seriousness, emotional intensity, and whether the expression normally evaluates the speaker, another person, an action, or a situation.

Do not assume a near-synonym is interchangeable merely because dictionary glosses overlap.

### 10.5 Conventional form

Default to the established form. Do not accidentally replace characters, reverse components, blend two idioms, or invent a plausible four-character phrase and present it as an existing idiom.

Deliberate idiom variation/活用 is allowed when:

- the base idiom is recoverable;
- the alteration has a clear rhetorical purpose;
- the character/narrator has reason to perform that alteration;
- the reader is not likely to mistake it for an error.

## 11. Idiom register, dialogue, and historical setting

Do not apply the crude rule `成语 = 书面语`.

Research on the `现代汉语词典（第7版）` idiom inventory shows that explicit style labels occur on only a small minority of four-character idioms. Individual idioms still differ in register, and some work naturally in conversation.

Therefore judge idiom use at the **entry level and speaker level**, not by category stereotype.

### Narration

Use an idiom when its conventional compression is the most natural expression. Do not stack idioms to signal literary quality, education, profundity, or "Chinese flavor".

Several idioms in one short paragraph may be correct individually and still create a prefabricated, essay-like surface.

### Dialogue

Ask:

- Would this speaker actually retrieve this idiom under these conditions?
- Does it fit their education, age, profession, region, social strategy, and emotional pressure?
- Is the phrase functioning as ordinary speech, quotation, teasing, bureaucratic formula, self-conscious eloquence, or performance?

A working-class character may naturally use common idioms; an academic may speak plainly. Do not assign idiom density directly from social class.

### Historical fiction

An idiom having an ancient source does **not** prove that its modern fixed form and modern meaning were available in the story period.

When period authenticity matters, verify:

- attested form;
- attested meaning;
- period/register;
- whether the expression is a later conventionalization of an older quotation.

Treat doubtful period idioms as research dependencies rather than improvising etymology.

## 12. Error severity and revision order

When reviewing Chinese prose, revise in this order:

1. **reference failure** — unclear who/what a zero form, pronoun, or noun phrase refers to;
2. **event/aspect failure** — wrong occurrence/completion/continuation/experience/change-of-state relation;
3. **construction failure** — malformed `把/被`, complement, modifier, or `的/地/得` relation;
4. **idiom semantic/syntactic misuse** — literal misreading, wrong slot, wrong target, wrong evaluative color, malformed fixed form;
5. **discourse relation failure** — connective scope or particle meaning contradicts the scene;
6. **register mismatch** — grammar or idiom is possible but wrong for narrator/speaker/situation;
7. **surface repetition** — pronouns, connectives, particles, corrective negatives, idioms, or four-character cadences recur visibly;
8. **pure rhythm tuning**.

Do not start at step 7 and accidentally damage steps 1-6.

For uncertain cases, preserve the original until meaning is established. Grammar review should not become synonym roulette.

For an advisory surface pass after semantic and grammatical review, run `scripts/prose_surface_audit.py <chapter-or-scene> --json`. Use its clipped event-negation, corrective-negation, explicit-connective, and clause-initial third-person-pronoun counts only to locate passages worth rereading. A high count is not a grammar verdict and must never trigger automatic rewriting; inspect reference clarity, event meaning, speaker voice, and local rhythm before changing prose.

During Skill maintenance, convert recurring Chinese-prose failures into rights-clean contrast families under `chinese-narrative-behavioral-evaluation.md` rather than expanding this grammar module with long example lists. Keep anchor/reversal evidence separate from ordinary Writer context.

## 13. Research basis

Research baseline: 2026-10. This module distills practical constraints from established modern-Chinese grammar, lexicography, discourse research, and idiom research. It intentionally converts scholarship into conservative writing rules rather than reproducing any source text.

Core references include:

- 吕叔湘主编，《现代汉语八百词》（增订本），商务印书馆。A grammar dictionary centered on function words and usage.
- 朱德熙，《语法讲义》；吕叔湘、朱德熙，《语法修辞讲话》；吕叔湘，《汉语语法分析问题》。Foundational modern-Chinese grammatical analysis.
- 中国社会科学院语言研究所词典编辑室，《现代汉语词典》（第7版），商务印书馆。Normative lexical reference; the Institute describes it as a dictionary compiled to establish modern-Chinese lexical norms.
- 商务印书馆辞书研究中心，《新华成语词典》（第2版）。Corpus-informed idiom dictionary with meaning, examples, evaluative color, and specific usage notes.
- `GB/T 15834-2011 标点符号用法`，全国语言文字标准化技术委员会，主管部门为教育部（语言）。Current national punctuation standard.
- 聂仁发，2001，《试论否定词“不”与“没有”的语义特征》，讨论 `不/没有` 在实现性、意愿和性状等方面的语义分工。
- 李铁根，2002，《“了”、“着”、“过”与汉语时制的表达》，讨论三类体/时相关形式与时间表达的关系。
- 朱素贞，1999，《现代汉语篇章及会话中零形回指的比较研究》；李姝姝，2020，《现代汉语篇章回指习得研究综述》。Support accessibility/topic continuity as central factors in noun/pronoun/zero-reference choice.
- 屈承熹，2008，《关联理论与汉语句末虚词的语篇功能》，讨论 `啊/呀、吧、呢` 等的情态与语篇功能。
- 张文贤，2017，《现代汉语连词的语篇连接功能研究》，北京大学出版社。Treats connective function as dependent on scope and register, including sentence- and discourse-level connection.
- 杨翠兰，2005，《汉语成语的语法功能研究》；夏秀文，2009，《基于语料库的汉语成语原型语法功能分析》。Support the view that idioms occupy multiple syntactic functions rather than one fixed slot.
- 李芋均、胡正旗，2012，《汉语成语的凝固、变异及其诱因》。Useful for distinguishing conventional fixedness from motivated variation.
- 张梁，2026，《汉语四字成语语体特征再考察——以〈现代汉语词典〉语体标记为中心》。Shows that a blanket `四字成语 = 书面语` rule is empirically too crude.

When scholarship disagrees on analysis, this Skill should not manufacture a single linguistic dogma. Prefer the narrower operational rule that protects meaning and naturalness while preserving authorial freedom.

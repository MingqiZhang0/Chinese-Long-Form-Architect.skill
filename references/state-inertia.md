# State Inertia and Earned Change

Use this reference when facts remain technically continuous but characters, relationships, institutions, reputations, places, or routines change too conveniently for the plot.

## Core principle

Continuity prevents contradiction. Inertia prevents plot-convenience transformation.

Treat consequential states as having persistence. A state continues because something sustains it; change requires pressure, context shift, accumulated strain, threshold crossing, or a shock strong enough to explain the new state. The new state then leaves residue and develops its own inertia.

Backstage model:

> prior state + sustaining forces + change forces + constraints -> next state + residue

This is a reasoning aid, not a literal equation.

## 1. Track why a state persists

Possible sources of inertia include:

- habit and automatic behavior;
- cost already paid;
- shame or face;
- legal record;
- bureaucracy and procedure;
- infrastructure;
- social expectation;
- emotional carryover;
- fear learned under earlier conditions;
- uncertainty or incomplete correction;
- sunk relationships and obligations;
- distributed responsibility;
- routine convenience;
- staffing, budgets, software, precedent, or logistics;
- public versions that are easier to repeat than the truth.

Do not assume a state persists simply because the author wants continuity.

## 2. Distinguish change modes

### Gradual
Repeated low-level pressure slowly changes behavior, belief, access, or routine.

### Thresholded
Pressure accumulates until a small final trigger becomes decisive.

### Context-switch
A pattern weakens because the cues, people, resources, or environment sustaining it disappear.

### Shock
Death, public exposure, disaster, undeniable evidence, arrest, collapse, betrayal, or another major event can change state abruptly.

Abrupt change still leaves aftermath.

## 3. Update different layers at different speeds

Do not make knowledge, emotion, habit, reputation, procedure, and infrastructure update in lockstep.

Examples of asynchronous change:

- a person accepts a fact before their body stops reacting as if the old danger exists;
- practical cooperation returns before intimacy;
- coworkers know a rumor is false before the official record changes;
- a road reopens before travel habits normalize;
- a leader changes policy before staff routines and incentives catch up;
- a public correction appears while search results, jokes, hiring decisions, and family assumptions still reflect the old version.

Use asynchronous update to create lived consequence, not artificial delay.

## 4. After change, check four things

### Residue
What survives from the old state?

### Lag
What has not updated yet?

### Rebound
What old pattern may return under stress, fatigue, old cues, or renewed incentives?

### Path dependence
How does the route taken constrain future options?

Two situations that look identical on a status sheet may not be the same if they were reached through different histories.

## 5. Domains of inertia

Activate only what matters.

- **physical/material** - injury, damage, contamination, repair, depletion, cleanup;
- **psychological** - fear, expectation, coping, self-story, conditioned response;
- **relational** - trust, resentment, dependence, care, debt, intimacy;
- **social/reputational** - labels, gossip, public memory, invitations, avoidance, prestige;
- **institutional** - records, precedents, procedures, jurisdiction, staffing, budgets;
- **epistemic** - first explanations, categories, theories, remembered testimony;
- **spatial** - routes, boundaries, place-memory, access habits;
- **economic** - contracts, prices, debt, supply chains, sunk investment;
- **cultural/normative** - etiquette, taboo, role expectations, inherited practice.

## 6. Psychological and relational transitions

Do not model character change as a single variable flip.

Ask separately:

- what they now believe;
- what they can admit;
- what they feel;
- what they do automatically;
- what they do under stress;
- what they expect from the other person;
- what behavior has become easier or harder.

A reconciliation can restore conversation while leaving caution. A betrayal can end trust while leaving care. A person can forgive without becoming willing to depend again.

Use `character-dynamics.md` for the deeper self-story and relationship model.

## 7. Institutions and public versions

An institution rarely updates merely because one person learns the truth.

Check:

- who has authority to change the record;
- what evidence standard applies;
- which department owns the category;
- who benefits from the old classification;
- whether informal practice changes before formal policy;
- whether old forms, software, or precedent continue reproducing the old state.

A public version can also become self-reinforcing. Once a reputation changes who is hired, trusted, mocked, invited, feared, obeyed, or blamed, later correction does not automatically restore the prior social world.

Use `organizational-learning.md` and `social-transmission-reputation.md` when these processes dominate.

## 8. Planning protocol for a consequential change

For any transition that matters later, know:

1. prior state;
2. what sustains it;
3. active pressure for change;
4. constraints on change;
5. update speed of each affected layer;
6. immediate result;
7. residue;
8. likely lag or rebound;
9. what future option now exists or disappears.

Do not show this framework to the reader unless the story naturally contains such analysis.

## 9. Anti-patterns

### Plot-synchronized healing
A wound, grief, fear, scandal, or estrangement vanishes exactly when inconvenient.

### Instant social reset
Proof arrives and everyone updates together.

### Permanent trait inertia
A character can never change because the author mistakes consistency for stasis.

### Fake path dependence
The text says history matters, but the same endpoint functions identically regardless of how it was reached.

### Inertia as padding
The story repeats old consequences without new pressure, reinterpretation, adaptation, or decision.

## Audit

- Why does the current state persist?
- What force is trying to change it?
- Which layer updates first?
- What lags behind?
- What old pattern can rebound?
- What residue remains after the apparent problem is solved?
- Would this transition still feel earned if the next plot beat were removed?

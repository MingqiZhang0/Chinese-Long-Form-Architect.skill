# Contingency, Noise, and Non-Teleological Life

> SELECTABLE POETICS CAPABILITY. Use when ordinary noise, chance, or anti-teleological texture serves the project. Do not impose one universal rule for coincidence across fairy tale, realism, mystery, absurdism, or other reality logics.

Use this reference when a tightly engineered plot begins to feel as if every object, delay, stranger, and accident exists only to serve the final conspiracy.

## Core principle

A causal world is not a world in which every event was narratively destined.

Allow bounded contingency so that characters live inside conditions they do not fully control. Then let consequences follow honestly.

## 1. Types of contingency

Useful forms include:

- weather;
- illness;
- equipment failure;
- ordinary human forgetfulness;
- delayed transport;
- timing collisions;
- misunderstanding;
- random inspection;
- unrelated local crisis;
- accidental encounter;
- stochastic variation in complex systems;
- a minor actor making an unanticipated choice for their own reasons.

Use these in proportion to the setting.

## 2. Chance may start or complicate; it should rarely solve

Strong use:

> A storm prevents the planned meeting, forcing both factions to improvise. Their improvisations reveal priorities and create new consequences.

Weak use:

> A storm kills the final antagonist and resolves the conflict.

Chance can alter the state. Characters must still own the consequential response.

## 3. Coincidence budget

Coincidences become suspicious when they are:

- highly improbable;
- extremely convenient;
- repeated in the same direction;
- required for resolution;
- unacknowledged by characters;
- unsupported by network, geography, routine, or prior conditions.

Naturalize necessary coincidences through shared institutions, routes, schedules, social networks, constrained geography, or repeated routines when truthful.

Do not over-explain every ordinary coincidence into secret causation.

## 4. Noise versus clue

Not every anomaly should be a clue.

Maintain three broad categories:

- **signal** - causally linked to a hidden process;
- **noise** - ordinary variation with no deeper plot function;
- **mixed** - ordinary event whose consequences later intersect the plot.

This prevents readers from learning that narrative attention always equals conspiracy.

## 5. False pattern formation

Characters may rationally infer the wrong pattern from noisy evidence.

A useful misinterpretation should have:

- enough observations to be plausible;
- a reason the actor weights those observations strongly;
- missing data or sampling bias;
- behavioral consequences;
- a path to correction.

Do not make wrong inference synonymous with stupidity.

## 6. Meaning after accident

An accidental event can become deeply meaningful because of how people interpret and respond to it.

Distinguish:

- **causal design** - someone intended the event;
- **narrative significance** - the event changes a life or theme;
- **retrospective meaning** - people later fit it into their self-story.

Meaning does not prove intention.

## 7. Anti-teleology reserve

Preserve some world remainder:

- relationships that never become plot keys;
- local problems that resolve locally;
- losses with no hidden mastermind;
- opportunities missed for mundane reasons;
- objects that remain merely objects.

This reserve makes true patterns harder to predict and the world less author-shaped.

## 8. Audit

- Did chance create the problem, change the problem, or solve the problem?
- Would the scene still matter if the coincidence were removed?
- Are too many unrelated details secretly connected?
- Does every misfortune trace to an intelligent enemy?
- Do characters ever misread noise as signal or signal as noise?
- Are accidents followed by durable consequences rather than dismissed after one chapter?
